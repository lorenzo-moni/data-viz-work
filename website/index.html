§ 03 — Our Reading

The Cemetery
& the Survivors

Of 6,425 games tracked, most are already silent. We measured who survived, and why — then left the dataset open for you to challenge our reading.

The median game keeps just 4% of its peak audience alive today. Half haven't been meaningfully played in months. Death is not the exception — it is the rule.

4% median alive ratio
9.1% median completion rate
6.6% median drop rate

Distribution of alive ratio across all games. The long tail to the right is vanishingly rare — only 348 titles (5%) remain above 20%.

§ Tag Signature

The DNA of Immortality

We compared the tag fingerprint of immortals (pre-2018, alive ratio > 10%) against mortals (alive ratio ≤ 3%). Eight tags rise; two fall — and they tell a coherent story.

"Immortals are open-ended, not finished." Worlds to build and mod, not shooters to complete.

Percentage-point difference in tag prevalence: immortals vs mortals. Positive = more common among survivors.

§ The Genre Battlefield

Genre Battlefield

The tag signal generalizes to whole genres. Some genres win systematically — not because they make better games, but because they are built for indefinite play.

Strategy, Simulation, and Massively Multiplayer sit at the top. Platformers and Puzzle games — designed to be finished — sit at the bottom.

Each dot is a game. Genres sorted by median alive ratio. The gap between "designed to end" and "designed to continue" is hard to miss.

§ The Critic Blind Spot

Communities See It First

Immortals are often underrated at launch. The rating delta — RAWG user score minus Metacritic score, normalized — skews positive for high-vitality games.

Communities recognize staying power before reviewers do. Critics score the launch; players score the years that follow.

Rating delta (user score − critic score) vs alive ratio. Positive delta = users rated higher than critics. Median delta for all games: −0.14.

§ The Structural Signal

Designed Not to End

There is a statistical signature behind the tag pattern. Games you can finish die faster than games you can't. Completion rate and alive ratio are negatively correlated (ρ = −0.175).

The genres that survive — MMO, Strategy, Simulation — are built without a finish line.

Completion rate (beaten/owned) vs alive ratio. Color by genre cluster. Genres in the top-left corner — high vitality, low completion — are the long-term survivors.

§ The Drop Rate Paradox

Not All Abandonment Is Equal

Some games are dropped because they are bad. Others because they are intense. The games where people quit and come back tend to survive.

High drop rate + high vitality: games you return to — roguelikes, survival, competitive titles. High drop rate + low vitality: genuinely abandoned.

Drop rate (dropped/owned) vs alive ratio. Lines mark the dataset medians. Four quadrants, four stories about why people stop playing.

§ The Price of Forever

Does Money Change the Equation?

We split games into price brackets and measured median vitality. The answer challenges the "free-to-play wins" narrative.

Counter-Strike: Source and Team Fortress 2 were paid games for years before going free. Price alone does not predict immortality.

Median alive ratio by launch price bracket (USD). Number of games per bucket shown above each bar.

§ The Cultural Signal

The Buzz Multiplier

Vitality isn't only a Steam number — it leaks into culture. Even normalizing for game age, immortals still receive 1.5× more YouTube videos per year than fading games (51,713 vs 34,459 median). The games that survive keep generating conversation.
